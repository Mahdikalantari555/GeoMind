# F3 — Trust & Provenance UX in Citation-Grounded AI Products

**Angle:** Trust/provenance UX patterns from Perplexity, Elicit, SciSpace (and adjacent citation-grounded products).
**Date:** 2026-11-03
**Audience fit:** GeoMind = local-first AI platform for remote sensing research; researchers/engineers need calibrated trust, not marketing trust.

---

## 1. Core concept split (Sanity glossary, foundational)

Citations, grounding, and provenance answer **three different questions**:

| Term | Question answered | UX implication |
|------|-------------------|----------------|
| **Grounding** | Did the system use real source content (vs. parametric memory)? | Internal design choice; invisible unless surfaced |
| **Citations** | Which part of that source content supports which part of the answer? | Visible trail, claim-level |
| **Provenance** | Where did the underlying content come from, who created it, when did it change, what happened to it? | Chain-of-custody record (SHA-256, asset_revision, segment) |

**GeoMind already has the provenance chain** (answer → citation → segment → asset_revision → objects/<sha256>). The gap is **citation UX** (claim-level chips, span-level linking) and **trust calibration** (showing grounding happened, exposing uncertainty).

## 2. Perplexity — layered citation stack (the reference)

Perplexity's trust model is "evidence in the reading flow": sourcing stays tied to claims as you scan, verification depth scales with skepticism.

### Layered stack (in order of depth)

1. **Inline domain chips** — publisher domain first (e.g. `northjersey +3`), placed at end of claims. Publisher-first labels build trust faster than footnote numbers. `+N` communicates multiple sources without cluttering.
2. **Research steps** — "Completed 2 steps" expands to "Searching the web" / "Checking current predictions". Plain-language, collapsed by default. Shows retrieval happened *before* claims appear (table stakes for search AI).
3. **Sources summary row** — favicon stack + "10 sources" beside share/copy/rewrite actions. Breadth at a glance, same elevation as action buttons (not footer debris).
4. **Chip popover** — click chip → title, snippet, 1/N pagination for multi-source claims. Quick-verify without leaving answer.
5. **Sources sidebar** — one-click panel from "10 sources" row; answer stays visible. Full card list (favicon, domain, title, snippet).
6. **Links tab** — peer tab beside Answer/Images; full audit view. Dedicated audit layer for power researchers.

### Feedback loop (critical)

Thumbs-down chips include **"Wrong sources"** as a first-class option, separate from "Inaccurate". This routes citation failures separately from writing failures — trains retrieval quality directly. Cost: no per-citation flagging from the modal (global only).

### Local audit

**"Check sources on a selection"** — highlight a passage → verify evidence for that span. Lets users audit one claim without re-running the whole query. Discoverability is select-first only (users may never find it).

### Takeaways for GeoMind

- Publisher/domain-first inline chips (not footnote numbers).
- Show retrieval happened (research steps in plain language).
- Source count + favicon stack on the answer chrome.
- Layered depth: chip popover → sidebar → full audit tab.
- "Wrong sources" in feedback taxonomy, separate from "inaccurate".
- Span-level "check sources on selection" for local audit.

## 3. Elicit — sentence-level citations for research

Elicit's core trust claim: "supports all AI-generated claims with **sentence-level citations** from the underlying sources." Transparency is a marketed pillar alongside accuracy.

### Patterns

- **Sentence-level citation markers** on every claim — not paragraph-level, not answer-level.
- **Extracted supporting quotes** displayed alongside claims (e.g. "supporting quotes on study design" shown in the screening UI).
- **Real-time reasoning transparency** — follow the agent's reasoning and methods as it works; inspect every claim.
- **Multi-source synthesis** — 1,000 papers / 20,000 data points at once; citations aggregate.
- **Customizable scope** — user changes which papers and what information is covered (transparency via control).
- **Zotero integration** — provenance extends into the user's reference manager (chain of custody leaves the product).

### Trust calibration angle

Elicit markets "most accurate AI product for scientific research" with validation studies — trust via evidence of accuracy, not just UX chrome. For GeoMind: pair UX patterns with measurable accuracy claims (abstention rate, citation precision).

## 4. SciSpace — document-grounded chat + literature review

SciSpace's strength: single-paper interaction. "Chat with PDF" answers are citation-backed and grounded in the paper's content; preset prompts cover common tasks.

### Patterns

- **In-line citations linking each claim to a specific source paper** in the indexed corpus (framed as grounding in indexed papers, not unsourced LLM training data).
- **Thematic grouping** of retrieved papers (not flat chronological/relevance list).
- **Extracted findings** pulled per paper against the specific question (not generic abstract summaries).
- **Preset prompts** for common tasks (summarize contributions, explain methodology) — reduces prompt engineering burden, standardizes verification.
- **Chat-with-PDF** scoped to one document — citation scope is bounded and legible.

### Weakness noted by reviewers

- Multi-paper comparison limited; mainly single-document workflow.
- Citations link to source paper (not span-level highlight in all flows).

## 5. Citation UX design patterns (cross-product consensus)

From AI UX Playground, ShapeofAI, Prodinja, Sanity, Edtek:

### Claim-level vs answer-level

- **Inline chips** answer "where did this sentence come from?" (claim-level).
- **Sources panel** answers "what did the system use overall?" (answer-level).
- Most grounded products need **both**. Progressive depth beats a single dense dump.

### Span-level citations (Adobe Acrobat AI, LlamaIndex CitationQueryEngine)

- Link a specific claim to a **specific passage** within the document.
- Clicking jumps to exact page and highlights the sentence.
- Requires storing character/token offsets for retrieved chunks; surfaces exact span wherever citation appears (inline, hover card, side panel).
- LlamaIndex approach: split retrieved chunks into sentence-sized pieces, label each individually → citation number maps to ~1–3 sentences.

### Hover preview (Copilot "citation chip")

- Tooltip on mouseover: source title + short excerpt + link to full source.
- Works on desktop; tap-to-expand on mobile.

### Side-by-side viewer (enterprise/legal/medical)

- Synchronized viewers highlight exact bounding boxes on scanned PDFs using web workers.
- Multi-source synthesis shows dual-pane comparison for contradictory facts.

### "Check sources" local audit

- Highlight passage → verify evidence for that span. Keeps user in product instead of opening rival tabs.

### Making broken/missing citations explicit

- If a source is unavailable, call it out directly. Empty states maintain trust; filler content erodes it.
- Don't hide gaps.

## 6. Trust calibration framework (the real craft)

From uxandai.substack, CleverX, tianpan.co:

### Core thesis

**Trust miscalibration is bidirectional.**
- **Over-trust** (automation bias): users accept wrong outputs without scrutiny. Looks like adoption. Dangerous.
- **Under-trust** (algorithm aversion): users discard correct outputs, duplicate effort. Looks like healthy skepticism. Kills adoption.

Goal = **calibrated trust**: confidence tracks actual reliability. Users rely on AI when right, override when wrong.

### Design implications for citation-grounded products

1. **Don't decorate benevolence.** Artificial typing delays, warm phrasing, "I" language don't reliably improve trust. They decorate competence without adding it.
2. **Expose uncertainty explicitly.** Abstention UI state (GeoMind already does this for LLM-unavailable). Extend to: "no supporting source found for this claim" → mark claim as unsupported rather than silently dropping or citing weakly.
3. **Graduated autonomy modes:** Suggest (AI shows options, no default) → Confirm (proposes action, waits for approval) → Auto (acts, logs for review). For GeoMind: answer generation could offer "draft answer" (suggest) vs "final answer" (auto) modes.
4. **Calibration measurement:** plot user trust ratings (1–7) against actual accuracy (ground truth). Perfect calibration = straight line. Track both trust (subjective) and accuracy (objective).
5. **Error detection rate** as the headline metric, not satisfaction. Elicit-style validation studies.

### Grounding spectrum (Perplexity, ziptie.ai)

Not every claim is equally grounded. Surface where a claim falls:

| Level | Looks like | Trust calibration |
|-------|-----------|-------------------|
| Fully cited | Inline citation number maps to specific retrieved source | Verify the cited source supports the specific claim |
| Synthesis-informed | No citation number, but specific/recent info | Cross-reference against cited sources in same answer |
| Uncited/parametric | No citation, generic statement | Treat as LLM memory; lowest trust |

GeoMind implication: mark abstention/uncertainty per claim, not just per answer. Don't let uncited claims ride along with cited ones invisibly.

## 7. Failure modes & accuracy data (calibration reality)

From arxiv 2605.06635 (citation evaluation in deep research agents):

- **Citation hallucination rates of 11–57%** persist across commercially deployed models.
- Citations may link to **inaccessible URLs**, reference **irrelevant content**, or **misrepresent** the source.
- Backward attribution: when a citation appears at end of a passage, it applies to all preceding uncited sentences — a common LLM practice that inflates apparent support.

### Verification pipeline (Sanity, 5 steps)

1. Retrieve with identifiers.
2. Ground (passage text alongside ids).
3. Generate with citation markers.
4. **Verify after generation** — check every identifier the model returned actually appears in retrieved set; check cited passage supports the sentence. Drop or flag failures.
5. Render source (title, link, quoted passage) so reader checks in one click.

**GeoMind implication:** Add a verification step between generation and render. Flag claims where citation doesn't entail claim. Surface "unsupported" explicitly rather than silently.

## 8. GeoMind-specific recommendations (startup-grade, research audience)

### Immediate (P0) — citation UX parity

1. **Inline citation chips** with source domain/asset name + count, placed at end of claims (not footnote list). For GeoMind: asset name + revision hash prefix, or collection name.
2. **Source summary row** on answer block: favicon/icon stack + "N sources" beside copy/share. Sets expectation before reading.
3. **Span-level linking**: citation click jumps to exact segment in source document/raster metadata, highlighting the passage. Requires character/token offsets (already have segments).
4. **Hover preview** (desktop) / tap-to-expand (mobile): source title + excerpt + link.
5. **"Wrong sources" feedback chip** separate from "inaccurate" in thumbs-down taxonomy.

### Medium (P1) — audit depth

6. **Sources sidebar** (one-click from count row) keeping answer visible.
7. **Full audit view** (peer tab/panel) with all references as scannable cards (title, URL/id, snippet, preview).
8. **"Check sources on selection"** — highlight passage → verify evidence for that span. Local audit.
9. **Research steps** in plain language before answer ("Retrieved N segments from M assets").

### Medium (P1) — trust calibration

10. **Per-claim grounding level** indicator (fully cited / synthesis-informed / uncited). Don't let uncited claims ride along invisibly.
11. **Abstention UI at claim level**, not just answer level: "no supporting source found" → mark claim unsupported.
12. **Verification step** between generation and render: flag claims where citation doesn't entail claim; surface "unsupported" explicitly.
13. **Graduated autonomy**: "draft answer" (suggest) vs "final answer" (auto) modes for high-stakes research.

### Longer (P2) — provenance depth (GeoMind's differentiator)

14. **Chain-of-custody panel**: answer → citation → segment → asset_revision → SHA-256, with timestamps and creator. This is GeoMind's moat vs. Perplexity (local, sovereign, auditable).
15. **Provenance export** (citation list with full chain) for reproducibility — researchers need this for papers.
16. **Zotero/export integration** (like Elicit) — provenance leaves the product into reference managers.
17. **Calibration dashboard**: plot user trust ratings vs. actual accuracy (ground truth). Track error detection rate as headline metric.

### What to skip

- Artificial typing delays / warm phrasing to "build trust" (decorates benevolence).
- Footnote-only citations at bottom of long answers (trust theater).
- Citation markers with no domain/source name visible.
- Feedback that can't distinguish bad sources from bad writing.
- Forcing users to open ten tabs to verify one claim.

## 10. Implications for GeoMind (one-line)

GeoMind's moat is the **auditable provenance chain** (answer→citation→segment→asset_revision→SHA-256) that Perplexity/Elicit/SciSpace cannot offer (they link to web URLs, not content-addressed local objects). The UX gap is **claim-level citation chips + span-level highlighting + per-claim grounding levels + verification step + calibration measurement**. Close the UX gap and the provenance chain becomes the differentiator.
