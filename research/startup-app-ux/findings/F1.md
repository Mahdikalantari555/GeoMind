# F1: User personas and jobs-to-be-done for remote-sensing AI platforms.

## Findings

### [1] Remote-sensing professionals in 2026 are hybrid sensor-expert + data-scientist + GIS-analyst roles spanning acquisition, processing, AI/ML, visualization, and domain collaboration.
- quote: "a remote sensing scientist/engineer in 2026 bridges the gap between raw sensor data and decision-ready information"
- url: https://www.refontelearning.com/blog/remote-sensing-scientist-engineer-in-2026-mastering-earth-observation-in-the-ai-era
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-01-29
- confidence: high

### [2] AI is the backbone (not an add-on) of Earth-observation analytics in 2026, automating feature extraction, object detection, and change analysis across massive datasets.
- quote: "In 2026, AI is no longer an add-on—it is the backbone of Earth Observation analytics."
- url: https://satpalda.com/state-of-earth-observation-in-2026
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026
- confidence: high

### [3] Primary EO end-users are governments, enterprises, humanitarian organizations, and researchers who need satellite-derived intelligence for environmental change, resource management, infrastructure monitoring, and crisis response.
- quote: "Governments, enterprises, humanitarian organizations, and researchers now rely on satellite-derived intelligence to understand environmental change, manage resources, monitor infrastructure, and respond to crises in near real time."
- url: https://satpalda.com/state-of-earth-observation-in-2026
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026
- confidence: high

### [4] End users demand actionable intelligence rather than raw imagery, pushing EO companies toward vertical-specific analytics, APIs, and decision-ready products.
- quote: "End users demand actionable intelligence, not raw imagery. As a result, EO companies focus on vertical-specific analytics, APIs, and decision-ready products."
- url: https://satpalda.com/state-of-earth-observation-in-2026
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026
- confidence: high

### [5] Foundation models are shifting EO from bespoke per-problem models toward reusable general-purpose models, potentially letting non-specialists use EO without a remote-sensing degree.
- quote: "you may not need a degree in remote sensing or spend tens or hundreds of thousands of dollars to take advantage of what EO can offer"
- url: https://newsletter.terrawatchspace.com/earth-observation-foundation-models-a-deep-dive
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-09-04
- confidence: medium

### [6] The geospatial labor market increasingly demands hybrids who connect geographic reasoning to code, cloud, EO, statistics, and an operational domain; 2025 O*NET postings showed ArcGIS 75%, Python 34%, SQL 22%.
- quote: "ArcGIS remained the dominant named software, appearing in 75 percent of those postings. But Python appeared in 34 percent and SQL in 22 percent."
- url: https://geoawesome.com/geospatial-workforce-ai-skills-spatial-judgment
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-08-15
- confidence: high

### [7] Spatial work is spreading across non-traditional titles (data scientist, imagery analyst, climate-risk analyst, product manager, solutions architect) even as "geographer" roles decline, indicating broadening personas.
- quote: "People doing geographic work increasingly have titles such as data scientist, imagery analyst, location-intelligence specialist, geospatial developer, climate-risk analyst, product manager or solutions architect."
- url: https://geoawesome.com/geospatial-workforce-ai-skills-spatial-judgment
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-08-15
- confidence: high

### [8] AI automates repetitive production tasks (digitization, scripting, first-pass classification, metadata) but validation judgment — scale, resolution, uncertainty, spatial dependence, provenance, bias — remains a human job.
- quote: "Someone still has to decide whether the source imagery is current enough, whether two datasets use compatible definitions, whether the scale supports the claimed conclusion..."
- url: https://geoawesome.com/geospatial-workforce-ai-skills-spatial-judgment
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026-08-15
- confidence: high

### [9] Geospatial AI platforms (e.g., ArcGIS) target invocable/autonomous agents, natural-language assistants, and trusted decision-making, anchored in security and governance.
- quote: "Deliver geospatial capabilities, reasoning, and intelligence through invocable and autonomous agents"
- url: https://architecture.arcgis.com/en/overview/introduction-to-arcgis/geospatial-ai.html
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [10] "Earth Intelligence" reframes EO value around decision-makers: transforming raw data into actionable insights by integrating Earth/social science, analytics, AI, and co-produced knowledge from local and Indigenous communities.
- quote: "Earth Intelligence is the next step in this process, transforming raw data into actionable insights by integrating Earth and social science, advanced analytics, artificial intelligence, and co-produced knowledge from local and Indigenous communities."
- url: https://earthobservations.org/resources/what-is-earth-intelligence
- source_type: primary
- published: unknown
- confidence: high

### [11] Open-data initiatives (Copernicus, Landsat) and open-source GIS are democratizing EO, bringing in non-traditional users such as small NGOs and local city planners.
- quote: "This democratization fosters innovation, lowers entry barriers, and drives new EO-based services across sectors."
- url: https://satpalda.com/state-of-earth-observation-in-2026
- source_type: secondary
- published: 2026
- confidence: high
